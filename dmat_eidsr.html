NMCP eIDSR Extract

Purpose. This tool extracts weekly surveillance data reported through the eIDSR (electronic Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response) system, covering clinical, confirmed positive and tested malaria, for every Hospital, Clinic, CHC, CHP and MCHP in the DHIS2 platform, cleans it, and produces tables that are ready for analysis. Every step below is applied automatically on each run.
1
Fetch the facility registry
All Level 5 (PHU) org units are pulled from DHIS2 with their full hierarchy, meaning District (Level 2) and Chiefdom (Level 4).
2
Apply exclusions before any data is fetched
A facility is dropped entirely, so it appears in no analysis, no denominator and no export, if it is:
  • A laboratory (name contains "Laboratory"). These are not health facilities, yet many are typed Hospital in the source, which would inflate the Hospital count.
  • A Marie Stopes facility , excluded by NMCP request (specialised SRH provider).
Only the five health facility types are kept, and the rule is strict: the last word of the facility name must be exactly Hospital, Clinic, CHC, CHP or MCHP. The one exception is Clinic: a name that contains the word Clinic anywhere is kept as a Clinic (for example "St John Clinic and Nursing Home"). Misspellings (such as "Climic") and names ending in "Home" without the word Clinic are excluded. Facilities with the same name under different parents (chiefdom or district) are separate facilities and both are included, because each is identified by its own OU ID.
The full list of excluded facilities, with the reason for each, is shown and downloadable after the run.
3
Fetch eIDSR data weekly
eIDSR malaria indicators are reported weekly (ISO epidemiological weeks) through the IDSR Weekly Disease Report dataset. Three data elements are fetched, each split by age group (<5 years / 5+ years) and by cases vs deaths:

3 data elements × 4 category option combos = 12 values fetched per facility per week. Each of the 12 stays split by age group (<5y or 5+y), because the epidemic threshold is computed separately for each of the 12, so an outbreak among children under five is never diluted by combining it with the older age group.
Unlike monthly HMIS forms, eIDSR periods are ISO weeks (Monday to Sunday), coded as YYYYWn (e.g. 2026W5). A year has 52 or 53 such weeks depending on where 1 January falls.
4
Identify every facility by its OU ID
Every facility in the platform is kept and identified by its DHIS2 OU ID, not by its name. Two facilities with the same name under different chiefdoms or districts are two different facilities, so both are captured, each with its own data and its own thresholds. The OU ID is included in every download, and where two facilities share a name the chiefdom is shown beside it in the facility dropdown. An Ownership column (Public or Private) is filled from the NMCP ownership list, matched on chiefdom and facility name; when a facility cannot be matched, the column shows TBD. The ownership list only fills this column and never removes a facility.
5
Classify facilities as Active or Inactive
  • Active , meaning it reported at least one eIDSR value during the selected period.
  • Inactive , meaning it is present in the DHIS2 registry but reported no eIDSR data at all for the period.
Only active facilities are used as the denominator in the reporting rates below, because an inactive facility is an existence problem, not a problem of reporting behaviour, and separating the two keeps both measurable.
6
Build a complete period grid
For every active facility, a row is created for every week of the selected range, including weeks with no data. Those appear explicitly with Reported = No rather than silently disappearing, so reporting gaps are visible and countable.
7
Calculate reporting rates
Reporting rate = (facilities reporting eIDSR that week ÷ ACTIVE facilities in that group) × 100
Computed per week, broken down by facility type (CHC, CHP, MCHP, Hospital, Clinic).
8
Set the baseline and the monitoring period two separate windows
Two windows are chosen on the Configure Extract tab. The baseline years (for example 2021 to 2025) are only used to learn what is normal for each facility. The monitoring weeks (for example 2026 W1 to the latest week) are the weeks that get checked against those thresholds. The baseline must end before the monitoring period begins, so a week is never compared against itself.
Both windows are fetched from DHIS2 in blocks of 13 weeks, which keeps each request small enough for the proxy even when five or more baseline years are selected.
9
Compute epidemic thresholds 5 methods · week specific
Thresholds are built separately for each facility, each series (Clinical, Confirmed Positive and Tested, split by <5y, 5+y and Combined, for Cases and for Deaths) and each epi week. For monitoring week w, the baseline set is the value that facility reported in week w of every baseline year. With 2021 to 2025 as the baseline, week 10 of 2026 is judged against week 10 of 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025, so the normal seasonal rise in the rainy season is built into the threshold.
MethodThreshold
Upper quartile (Q3)The 75th percentile of the baseline set, which is the upper bound of the interquartile range
95th percentile (P95)The value below which 95% of the baseline set falls
MeanThe average of the baseline set, which is the C SUM style line
Mean + 2 SDAverage plus two standard deviations
Mean + 3 SDAverage plus three standard deviations
  • A monitoring week is flagged for a method when its value is greater than that method's threshold. The Status column summarises this as Alert above Mean + 3 SD, Warning above Mean + 2 SD, and Normal otherwise.
  • A Weekly Thresholds file exported earlier can be loaded back on the Configure Extract tab. The baseline years are then not fetched at all, and the thresholds in that file are applied to whatever weeks you fetch.
  • Weeks a facility did not report are left out of the baseline set rather than counted as zero, so reporting gaps do not pull the threshold down.
  • One baseline value is enough. With a single value the SD is 0, so all five thresholds equal that value.
  • If a week has no baseline value at all, the threshold is borrowed from the nearest earlier week (for week 5, check W4, then W3, down to W1). If no earlier week has a value, the nearest later week is used (W6, W7, up to W53). The week used is shown in the Baseline Week Used column.
  • If the facility has no baseline value for that series in any week, every threshold is set to 0 so the line can still be plotted, and the week is marked No baseline instead of being flagged.
  • Week 53 uses week 52 of the baseline years when a baseline year has no week 53.
  • The standard deviation is the sample SD (n minus 1), and Q3 uses linear interpolation, matching R quantile(type = 7) and pandas.
How the five compare. The Mean line is the most sensitive and flags the most weeks, so it works as an early warning. Q3 sits a little higher and is the method many malaria programmes use when only a few baseline years exist, while P95 sits close to the top of the baseline and only flags weeks higher than almost every past value for that week. Mean + 2 SD is the usual alert line, and Mean + 3 SD is the strictest, flagging only clear departures from normal. All five are computed on every run; the Epidemic Threshold tab lets you switch between them.
Testing the thresholds on past years. The Year filter on the Epidemic Threshold tab also lists each baseline year. Choosing one applies the same week specific thresholds to every week of that year, so you can see how often each method would have flagged in a normal year. Because that year is itself part of the baseline, this is an in sample check: the Mean line will naturally be crossed in roughly half of the weeks, while Mean + 2 SD and Mean + 3 SD should rarely be crossed if they are well calibrated.
10
Produce the outputs
The monitoring file has one row per facility per week. Each series carries its value, the baseline N, the five thresholds (Q3, P95, Mean, Mean + 2 SD, Mean + 3 SD), the baseline SD, and an Above threshold column that lists every method the week exceeded. Two more files come with it: the raw baseline year data (one row per facility per week per year) and a weekly threshold file (one row per facility for every epi week of the year, no year column) holding the value of every method for every series. The flagged weeks download separately too. Other analyses include weekly reporting rates by facility type, facility activity, excluded facilities and yearly totals, all for the monitoring period and all downloadable as CSV or Excel.

Facility Weekly Data
Monitoring weeks. One row per facility per week. Each of the 18 series (Clinical/Positive/Tested × <5y/5+y/Combined × Cases/Deaths) carries its value, baseline N, Q3, P95, Mean, Mean + 2 SD, Mean + 3 SD, baseline SD and the methods it exceeded.
Baseline Year Data
The raw baseline years. One row per facility per week per year, with the weekly value of each of the 18 series. These are the values the thresholds were built from.
Weekly Thresholds
One row per facility for every epi week of the year (W1 to W52, or W53 where a baseline year has one), no year column. A Facility No column numbers the facilities 1 to N, so the last number is how many facilities are in the file. For each of the 18 series: baseline N, the week used, and the value of all five methods (Q3, P95, Mean, Mean + 2 SD, Mean + 3 SD) plus the SD.

Method: week specific thresholds from the baseline years
For each facility, series and epi week, the same week in every baseline year (baseline) forms the baseline set. Five thresholds come from that set: Upper quartile (Q3, 75th percentile), 95th percentile (P95), Mean, Mean + 2 SD and Mean + 3 SD. Each monitoring week (monitoring) is flagged for a method when its value is above that method's threshold. One baseline value is enough. A week with no baseline borrows the nearest earlier week, or failing that the nearest later week; with no baseline at all the threshold is 0 and the week is not flagged. Pick the method below to change the threshold line on the charts.
