NMCP Confirmed Malaria Cases Extract

Purpose. This tool extracts monthly confirmed malaria cases: test positive results only (RDT and Microscopy), from DHIS2 for every health facility in Sierra Leone, from the old form, the new HF01 facility form and community reporting. It cleans the data, attaches Public/Private ownership, and produces analysis-ready tables. Every step below is applied automatically on each run.
1
Fetch the facility registry
All Level 5 (PHU) org units are pulled from DHIS2 with their full hierarchy, District (Level 2) and Chiefdom (Level 4).

Level 6 CHWs are also pulled. Community tests are reported at Level 6, not at the facility, so each Level 6 unit is mapped to its Level 5 PHU parent and its values are rolled up to that facility. Level 6 units never appear as facilities in their own right, they only contribute community cases to their parent PHU.
2
Apply exclusions before any data is fetched
A facility is dropped entirely, appearing in no analysis, denominator, or export, if it is:
  • A laboratory (name contains "Laboratory"). These are not health facilities, yet many are typed Hospital in the source (e.g. "Bo Regional Hospital Laboratory"), which would inflate the Hospital count.
  • A Marie Stopes facility: excluded by NMCP request (specialised SRH provider).
  • Of an unrecognised type: the type cannot be derived from the facility name.
Facility types are normalised to five categories: Hospital, Clinic, CHC, CHP, MCHP. Misspellings are folded in ("Climic" → Clinic), as are nursing homes ("Home" → Clinic).

The computation
Confirmed at facility <age> = RDT Positive + Microscopy Positive
Confirmed at community <age> = RDT Positive, CHWs
Confirmed overall <age> = at facility + at community

Confirmed: RDT only = facility RDT+  +  community RDT+
Two confirmed series, and why. Facility microscopy positives are age disaggregated on both forms, so the per age RDT + Microscopy figures are complete and consistently defined throughout, before and after the cutover. A RDT only series is still produced alongside them, because it is a single method available at every facility including those with no laboratory, which makes it the better series for comparing places rather than periods. Both are in every export, and the Series Break Check chart plots them together so any step can be inspected.
9 series are cleaned independently: facility RDT+, facility Microscopy+ and community RDT+, each across the 3 age groups. Outlier detection runs on each series separately.
Microscopy is excluded for MCHP and CHP. Microscopy needs a microscope and a laboratory, and neither an MCHP nor a CHP has one, so any microscopy value they report is skipped: it never enters the data, the analyses or any export. Such entries are almost certainly data entry errors, most often an RDT result recorded in the microscopy column. Hospital, CHC and Clinic may report microscopy and their values are kept in full. Every skipped record is listed, with its facility and value, in the Wrong Reporting tab.

4
Attach ownership (Public / Private / In DHIS2 not in master)
Each facility is matched to the NMCP PHU ownership master list, in this order:
  1. DHIS2 OU ID: exact, and immune to renames or spelling changes
  2. Chiefdom (Level 4) + Facility name (Level 5)
  3. Facility name alone
In the master list but not in DHIS2 is a reference list only. There is no DHIS2 org unit, so there is no data to extract and no calculation is performed for these facilities. They appear in no total, no denominator, no reporting rate and no case export. They are listed by name in the Ownership Reconciliation report so the master list can be corrected at source, and nothing else.
In DHIS2 but not in the master list means ownership is Unknown, and nothing more. The facility is still fetched, its data still extracted, and its cases still counted in every district, facility type, age group and national total. Only the ownership label is affected. Unknown is a classification, not an exclusion, and it is shown as a third class beside Public and Private wherever ownership is reported, so Public plus Private plus Unknown always reconciles to the total. The facilities concerned are listed in the Ownership Reconciliation report as Unmatched, so the master list can be corrected at source.

5
Detect and flag outliers IQR method, flag only
Applied independently to each facility × each year × each age group. Only reported values (non-missing, greater than zero) are used.
Q1 = 25th percentile  ·  Q3 = 75th percentile  ·  IQR = Q3 − Q1
Upper bound = Q3 + 3.0 × IQR   →   anything above it is flagged and left exactly as reported
Worked example: one facility, 0-59m, 12 months of one year
Step A · The 12 monthly values, sorted
110, 115, 120, 125, 128, 130, 132, 135, 140, 145, 150, 900
Step B · Find the quartiles (they split the sorted data)
Q1 (25th pct)123.75a quarter of months fall below this
Median (50th pct)131the facility's typical month
Q3 (75th pct)141.25three quarters fall below this
Step C · Measure the normal spread
IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 141.25 − 123.75 = 17.5
The middle 50% of this facility's months span just 17.5 cases: it is a steady facility.
Step D · Set the fence
Upper bound = Q3 + 3.0 × IQR = 141.25 + 3.0 × 17.5 = 193.75
Step E · Flag it
900 > 193.75  →  Outlier Flag = Yes  →  the value stays 900 and is listed in the Outliers tab for checking in DHIS2
All other 11 months sit below the fence and are left completely untouched.
The same thing, drawn
What is NOT touched
  • Zeros stay zero. A facility that genuinely saw 0 patients keeps its 0, it is never flagged, and never used to compute the bound or the median.
  • Missing stays missing. A month with no report stays blank; the tool never invents a value for it.
  • Fewer than 4 reported values? No IQR bound is computed (quartiles would be unstable). The absolute threshold still applies.
  • Nothing at all is altered. Detection is flag only: every total, analysis and export uses the value exactly as reported in DHIS2. A flag is a request to check the entry at source, not a change to the data.
Why 3.0 and not 1.5? Tukey calls 1.5×IQR an "outlier" fence and 3.0×IQR a "far outlier" fence. The wider fence keeps the flag list short enough for someone to actually work through. Malaria is seasonal, so a rainy season month is legitimately two or three times a dry season month, and a narrow fence would fill the list with those real peaks. 3.0 lets them through and reserves the flag for values that are almost certainly data entry errors, such as a stray digit or a duplicated entry.

Why is the median still shown? Nothing is replaced, but the facility median is reported alongside each flagged value because it tells you what a normal month looks like at that facility. That is usually all someone needs to see whether the flagged figure is credible or a typing error.
6
Classify facilities as Reported or did not report
  • Active: reported at least one confirmed case during the selected period.
  • Inactive: present in the DHIS2 registry but reported no confirmed cases at all for the period.
Example: why inactive facilities are removed from the denominator
Suppose a group has 4 private facilities. Facility D never reports at all in the period:
FacilityReported at least once?Status
AYesActive
BYesActive
CYesActive
DNo: never, all periodInactive
In a month where all 3 active facilities reported:
If all 4 were counted:   3 ÷ 4 = 75%: this reads as a reporting failure
Facilities that reported only (the method used here):   3 ÷ 3 = 100%: every functioning facility did report
Facility D is not a reporting problem; it is an existence problem. Separating the two keeps both measurable: activity is reported in its own analysis, and the reporting rate stays a clean measure of reporting behaviour.
7
Build a complete period grid
For every active facility, a row is created for every month of the selected range: including months with no data. Those appear explicitly with Reported = No rather than silently disappearing, so reporting gaps are visible and countable.
Example: a facility that did not report in June

June is kept as a row with no value and Reported = No. The facility therefore contributes 12 rows for the year, not 11.
This is what makes reporting gaps countable: a month that is absent from the data cannot be measured. Keeping it visible means June correctly lowers that month's reporting rate.
8
Calculate reporting rates
Reporting rate = (facilities reporting tests that month ÷ ACTIVE facilities in that group) × 100
Computed per month, broken down by ownership (Public, Private, In DHIS2 not in master) and by facility type (CHC, CHP, MCHP, Hospital, Clinic).
Example: March, Public facilities
Public facilities that reported in the period: 1,200
Public facilities that reported tests in March: 1,044

Reporting rate = 1,044 ÷ 1,200 × 100 = 87.0%
Read as: of the public facilities that actually function, 87% submitted testing data in March. The other 13% are genuine reporting gaps worth chasing.
9
Produce the outputs
Each age group carries three columns, in this order:
  • Value: the value as reported in DHIS2. This is what every total and analysis uses.
  • IQR Upper Bound: the threshold it was tested against (Q3 + 3.0 × IQR), blank where there were fewer than 4 reported months
  • Outlier Flag: Yes if it exceeded the IQR bound or 5,000 in that single facility month
There is no Corrected column, because nothing is corrected. The flag marks a value for review so it can be fixed at source in DHIS2. Until it is fixed, the reported value is what the tool reports.
Plus derived Confirmed per age group: at facility, at community and overall, and a RDT only series that is comparable across the April 2026 cutover.
